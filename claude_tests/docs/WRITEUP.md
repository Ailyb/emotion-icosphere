# What linear emotion probes on LLM hidden states do and do not buy you: an audit

*Draft, 2026-11-17. Every number below is traceable to a committed report (paths in brackets) and to the pre-registration written in `PLAN_v2.md` before the corresponding run. Citations are from memory and must be verified before release (see the list at the end). This is a hobby-scale, non-commercial study of three small 4-bit open models; it makes no claim about model feelings or internal states.*

## Abstract

Linear "emotion probes" read emotion-concept directions out of an open-weight language model's residual stream and are a popular basis for visualisations and monitors. We ask a narrow question: what do such probes carry beyond cheap baselines, and where? We audit probes on three small instruction-tuned models (Llama-3.2-3B, Gemma-2-2B, Mistral-7B; all 4-bit) with a frozen, hash-verified human test set (GoEmotions, n=840), three additional human-labelled sources (tweets, event descriptions, dialogue), pre-registered hypotheses and decision rules, paired bootstrap intervals, and baselines trained on the *same* data (TF-IDF, a bag of the model's own input embeddings, and a trained classifier on the same hidden states). Four results hold on all three models. (1) In-domain, a TF-IDF classifier beats the probe by about 0.09 macro-F1 (probe − TF-IDF −0.083 to −0.088, intervals exclude 0), and a 7B model does not close the gap. (2) Cross-domain the order reverses: trained on the same data, the probe beats TF-IDF on all three external sources (+0.054 to +0.190), and by up to +0.264 when trained only on LLM-written text. (3) A linear read-out is sufficient: an MLP and a valence-hierarchy do not improve on logistic regression. (4) The probes' z-scores are not comparable across text length: on neutral human text 15–83% of passages raise some |z|>3 depending on length, and per-length recalibration is needed. Probe-direction geometry correlates with human valence–arousal–dominance distances between emotion words on two of three models. Human training data, not representation or pooling choices, is the main lever.

## 1. Question and design

Probes of this kind are typically built from LLM-generated emotional passages, then shown to separate held-out passages from the same generator. That is weak evidence: such corpora are often lexically solvable (our first corpus: TF-IDF 0.953 vs probe 0.857 [`docs/FINDINGS.md`]). We therefore test probes against (a) a TF-IDF classifier trained on the same data, (b) a logistic regression on the mean of the model's input token embeddings (a "bag of embeddings" that has the model's vocabulary knowledge but no context), (c) a logistic regression on the same hidden states as the probe (the linear ceiling), and (d) a shuffled-label null. Every comparison is paired (same test items), reported as a bootstrap 95% interval, and each experiment's hypotheses and decision rules were fixed in `PLAN_v2.md` before the run.

**Probes.** One direction per emotion (joy, sadness, fear, anger, surprise, disgust, love) fitted by shrinkage LDA, logistic regression or mean difference on mean-pooled mid-layer states (first 4 tokens skipped), z-scored against held-out neutral text, with a neutral threshold chosen on validation. Layer, estimator and threshold are chosen on validation only. The extractor uses a causal mask and is verified against each model's own forward pass (relative difference 3e-4 to 6e-4); it handles Gemma's embedding scaling.

**Data.** Training: LLM-written passages from two writers (Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen3.8-27B), plus human GoEmotions comments (up to 378 per class, official train split only). Test: (i) a frozen GoEmotions test set (official test split, single-label items, near-duplicates of train/dev removed, n=840, class counts 63–151, SHA-256 manifest; the evaluator refuses a modified file) [`data/testsets/*.manifest.json`]; (ii) dair-ai/emotion test (tweets, hashtag-derived labels), ISEAR (first-person events) and DailyDialog (utterances). Class sets differ across sources, so predictions are restricted to each source's classes. Calm exists only in LLM-written text (writer-confounded) and is excluded from all headline numbers.

## 2. Results

**2.1 In-domain, lexical features win [`frozen_eval.md`].** On the GoEmotions frozen set (8-way, chance 0.125): Llama probe 0.510 [0.476, 0.544], Gemma 0.513 [0.478, 0.546], Mistral 0.515 [0.481, 0.547]; TF-IDF 0.599 [0.564, 0.630]; bag of embeddings 0.394–0.403; trained classifier on the same states 0.521 / 0.537 / 0.559. Probe − TF-IDF: −0.088, −0.085, −0.083 (all intervals exclude 0). Probe − bag of embeddings: +0.107, +0.112, +0.121 (all exclude 0). The probe's recall on neutral text is low (0.19 on Llama).

**2.2 Cross-domain, the probe wins [`external_eval.md`].** Trained on the same GoEmotions-derived data and tested on other human sources (probe − TF-IDF, macro-F1 [95% CI], pre-registered H1): Llama dair +0.151 [+0.122, +0.178], ISEAR +0.132 [+0.101, +0.163], DailyDialog +0.103 [+0.077, +0.130]; Gemma +0.137, +0.190, +0.095; Mistral +0.152, +0.127, +0.054 (all exclude 0). Trained only on LLM-written text the gaps are larger: Llama +0.167, +0.262, +0.125; Gemma +0.127, +0.264, +0.152; Mistral +0.135, +0.196, +0.092. With *no* human training data the probes score 0.305–0.313 on the frozen human set (shuffle-null p95 ≈ 0.13) where TF-IDF scores 0.146. Reading: lexical models fit the vocabulary of their training domain; probes transfer better because hidden states carry information that is not tied to that vocabulary. Caveats: dair labels are hashtag-derived and lexical; ISEAR provenance is unverified; sources differ in class sets.

**2.3 The lever is human training data, not the probe [`frozen_eval.md`].** Adding human rows (0 → 378 per class) raises frozen-set F1 steadily (native LR k=378 vs k=80: +0.069 [+0.039, +0.099] Llama, +0.103 Gemma, +0.096 Mistral; pre-registered rule: data-limited). Once human data is present, the LLM-written rows add nothing measurable (human-only training equals LLM+human). The trained classifier on the same states is statistically equal to the probe on most sets, i.e. the single-direction restriction costs little (exceptions: DailyDialog utterances; Mistral on the frozen set, −0.044 [−0.070, −0.019]).

**2.4 Linear is enough; geometry is human-like on two models [`t8_experiments.md`].** A valence-hierarchy beat flat logistic regression on at most 1 of 4 sets and an MLP on 0 of 4 (rule: ≥ 3 of 4), on all three models. The 21 pairwise dissimilarities of the probe directions correlate with pairwise valence–arousal–dominance distances between the seven emotion words (NRC VAD v2.1): Spearman ρ = +0.53 / +0.60 (Llama, lda / mean-diff), +0.61 / +0.63 (Gemma), all p ≤ 0.016 under an exact permutation over 5040 labelings. On Mistral the mean-difference directions agree (+0.54, p=0.005) but the validation-selected logreg directions do not (+0.31, p=0.112), so the pre-registered rule is not met there. Seven points; nominal.

**2.5 Pooling and prompting [`variants.md`].** Mean-pooling is a sound default. Last-token and last-4 pooling are worse on most sets (by up to −0.15; last-token is equal on two Llama sets); max-pooling and concatenating plateau layers are equivalent. A prompted read-out helped on two of four sets on Llama (ISEAR +0.136, DailyDialog +0.072) but not on Gemma (0 of 4); not a finding.

**2.6 The z-scores are not length-calibrated [`length_calibration.md`].** On 5,014 held-out neutral human comments the share of passages with any |z|>3 under the original calibration is 76.0% (1–2 words) to 15.3% (20+ words) on Llama, 83.4% to 20.1% on Gemma, 69.0% to 15.3% on Mistral. Per-bucket cross-validated recalibration reduces this to about 1–1.7% per emotion and 4.0–7.4% per passage, but misses the pre-registered bounds (≤ 2% per emotion, ≤ 5% per passage upper confidence limit) in every bucket for all three models because the tails are heavier than Gaussian. Descriptively, after recalibration one threshold of about 2.8–3.3 on max |z| keeps false alarms near 5% at every length, whereas the unmodified scores need 3.7–10.9 depending on length. The earlier anecdote ("The sky is blue." scoring sadness z=+4.9) is an instance.

## 3. What this does and does not show

Supported: emotion directions in mid-layer states carry information beyond a bag of the model's own embeddings (all models, all human sets); they transfer across human text domains better than a lexical classifier trained on the same data; the information is linear at the chosen layer; the probe geometry is related to human affect norms (two of three models).

Not supported: that probes beat lexical baselines in-domain on human text (they do not); that scores are comparable across lengths (they are not); that LLM-written emotion corpora are sufficient (probes trained on them transfer at about 0.31 macro-F1 versus 0.51–0.57 with human data); that a larger model fixes the in-domain gap (7B does not); that prompting or other pooling helps. Nothing here supports statements about a model's feelings or internal emotional state: the probes are linear read-outs correlated with emotion-concept text.

## 4. Limitations

Three small 4-bit models from three families (2B–7B); no larger or full-precision model. Human evidence comes from reddit comments plus three sources of mixed provenance (hashtag labels, unverified ISEAR copy, utterances without context); GoEmotions labels are noisy and the single-label rule keeps items with weaker rater agreement (strict unanimity left 8–29 items per class). Class sets differ across sources, so macro-F1 values are not comparable across sources. A hand-written test set that we initially used was accidentally committed to the public repository; we treat all results on it as non-blind and base conclusions on the frozen and external sets. We report 16–20 paired comparisons per model per experiment with unadjusted intervals; the ≥3-of-4 consistency rules are the guard, and some results (prompted read-out, geometry on Mistral) did not replicate. The post hoc analyses (fusion with TF-IDF, lexicon strata, alarm thresholds) are labelled as such where reported. The SAE analysis planned for Gemma (Gemma Scope SAEs were trained on the base model, not the instruction-tuned one) and activation-steering tests are not done.

## 5. Reproducibility and compliance

Code, tests (17 model-free synthetic tests, passing from a clean checkout), pre-registrations and reports are in the repository. `probekit_audit.py` reproduces the headline table from cached activations exactly (Llama probe 0.510, native LR 0.521, bag of embeddings 0.403, TF-IDF 0.599). Third-party data stay outside the repository: GoEmotions (Apache-2.0) is rebuilt from the original release by hash-checked scripts; dair-ai/emotion (license "other", no terms stated), the Hub copy of ISEAR (original host unreachable; uploader's license tag unverified) and DailyDialog (CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0) are rebuilt locally from manifests; the NRC EmoLex and VAD lexicons (non-commercial research use, no redistribution) are used locally and never committed. Our code and documents are released under The Unlicense (public domain); `NOTICE.md` lists the third-party terms that remain.
